\abstract{%
Artificial-intelligence systems can retrieve a well-supported fact yet apply it
to the wrong time, scope, epistemic state or possible world. We call this
failure \emph{situation blindness} and define \emph{situation engineering} as
constructing the query-relative state that governs evidence applicability. We
introduce a state interface, Situation-Catching Question Answering, and
SituationCatch-Bench, a 4,200-item diagnostic spanning seven applicability
categories from temporal change to counterfactual worlds. A deterministic implementation satisfies every invariant under
oracle states; accuracy falls to 66.5\% under text-inferred states and 45.1\%
after corruption, and each matched ablation produces a failure confined to its
category.
These results establish an executable diagnostic, not language-model
superiority. Across nine models from seven developers, including two
pre-registered replications, explicit situation prompting never detectably beats matched
structured prompting, whereas identical claims without timestamps fail on
time-dependent items and dating natural temporal questions adds 22--27 points.
Language-model sensors recover most cues a rule-based sensor misses, but not
information absent from text.
}

\keywords{large language models, situation engineering, evidence applicability, situation awareness, hallucination, trustworthy artificial intelligence, temporal reasoning, question answering, agent systems}
