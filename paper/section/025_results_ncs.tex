\section*{Results}

\subsection*{A controlled benchmark isolates seven applicability invariants}

SituationCatch-Bench v0.2 contains 4,200 generated English instances, with 600
instances in each of seven diagnostic categories: temporal state change,
omitted answer-critical premises, source conflict, proposed versus confirmed
events, local versus universal scope, observer-dependent knowledge and real
versus counterfactual worlds. Every item provides a question, shuffled claims,
query time, required state variables, a gold action and, where answerable, a
gold answer. The benchmark is intentionally transparent: each category is a
software-level test of one applicability invariant rather than a claim to
natural linguistic diversity. Within every category the gold answers are
balanced and, wherever claim order could decide the answer, the decisive claim
is the most recent one in exactly half of the items, so neither a constant
answer nor a recency rule can exceed chance on the decisive variable (Methods).
Version 0.1 lacked this balancing; it is archived with its results.

\subsection*{Oracle-state control satisfies the generated invariants}

SCQA was compared with lexical retrieval, recency-ranked retrieval and a
latest-mention rule. All methods received the same generated claims; SCQA alone
used the complete oracle situation state and transition semantics. SCQA obtained
100.0\% action-and-answer accuracy. Lexical retrieval obtained 54.3\%, recency
ranking 50.0\% and latest mention 47.4\% (Fig.~\ref{fig:accuracy}). These
numbers are deterministic consequences of the benchmark and implementation and
should be interpreted as regression-test coverage, not as estimates of model
performance in a population of natural questions.

The control values are structurally induced. Each control reads one selected
claim at face value, and because every category is balanced, such a reading is
right about half of the time; the gaps to SCQA are therefore not presented as
empirical effect sizes against competitive baselines. The one exception is
lexical retrieval on counterfactual items (100\%): the question names the
hypothetical scenario, so content-word overlap selects the hypothetical claim.

The category-level pattern provides the useful result (Fig.~\ref{fig:category}).
All three controls answer hidden-premise items without asking and score 25\%,
being right only when a stated premise makes the answer yes. On temporal items
they fail whenever the query time precedes the latest event, so recency ranking,
which always selects the newest appointment, scores exactly 50\%. On
source-conflict and counterfactual items recency is right only when the decisive
claim happens to be the newest (50\%), and a status-blind reading of modality
items is right on every confirmed closure but on only half of the undecided
ones (about 75\%). Thus a single aggregate ``hallucination'' label hides
distinct and testable mechanisms.

\subsection*{Component ablations produce matched failure signatures}

Removing any one of temporal validity, source status, modality, scope, observer
knowledge, world identity or missing-variable detection produces exactly 300
errors, all within the matching category and none elsewhere
(Fig.~\ref{fig:ablation}). In v0.1 the source and world ablations produced no
errors, because the decisive claim was always also the most recent; the
balanced generator removes that confound, so every ablation now tests its
variable. This causal alignment is the main empirical contribution of the
controlled study. It shows that the implementation does not
merely benefit from a generic instruction to be cautious; behaviour changes
when the specific variable required by an item is removed. Because benchmark
and solver share the same schema, the result is best viewed as a mechanistic
unit test that future neural systems should pass under both gold and predicted
states.

\subsection*{Partial observability exposes a sensing gap}

We evaluated two observability regimes. In the evidence-complete control, all
required variables are supplied in the structured state. In the text-only
regime, the sensor must reconstruct a state from the rendered claims; variables
not expressed in those claims remain unobservable rather than being silently
copied from gold metadata. Across
all 4,200 diagnostic instances, action-and-answer accuracy fell from 100.0\%
with gold states to 66.5\% with predicted states. Deliberately overwriting
source status, modality and scope (which also carries world identity) in the
predicted state reduced accuracy further to 45.1\%. These are complete executions, not projected values. They
show that the oracle result is not preserved when situation sensing is
imperfect. Because the texts are generated from the same seven scenario
families and the sensor is a transparent rule baseline, these results remain
controlled diagnostics rather than evidence of natural-language or LLM
generalization, and a partial-observability stress test rather than an estimate
of extraction from evidence-complete prose (Methods).

Claim-level scoring localizes the sensor's limitations
(Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:slot-sensing}). Event type reached 92.3\%, modality 100.0\%,
scope 61.5\%, actor 30.8\% and joint source/status 34.6\%. Temporal expressions
and validity intervals scored 0\% because the generated claim strings omit the
numeric times stored in the hidden schema: a system cannot reconstruct a state
variable that is neither expressed nor retrieved. Observer and world slots
reached 92.3\% and 100.0\% under repetitive generated language and are not
estimates of natural extraction. In this deterministic pipeline, 1,407 final
errors are attributable to sensing, zero to update/policy under gold state, and
generation is a fixed renderer.


\subsection*{Progressive stress testing separates execution, sensing and validation}

Read as a three-stage stress test, the complete benchmark produces
$100.0\%\rightarrow66.5\%\rightarrow45.1\%$: an oracle state tests execution,
a text-derived state adds sensing error, and targeted corruption tests whether
unvalidated state can propagate into the answer. The corresponding losses are
33.5 and 21.4 percentage points. This progression is empirical in the limited
sense that every stage was executed on all 4,200 items and item-level outputs
are archived. It is not a learning curve, a comparison of three models or an
estimate of robustness to naturally occurring corruption. In particular, the
last stage is an intentionally severe intervention selected to damage source,
modality and scope fields; monotonic degradation is therefore a diagnostic
expectation rather than an independently discovered population effect.

\subsection*{Natural-language benchmarks establish prevalence, not SCQA efficacy}

Independent resources show that situation dependence is not confined to the
generated benchmark. SituatedQA reported that approximately 16.5\% of sampled
NQ-Open questions depended on time or place and that updated evidence did not
remove a substantial present-time performance gap~\cite{zhang2021situatedqa}.
FreshQA, PAT-Questions, TimeQA, ChronoQA and TDBench test related problems in
freshness, evolving facts and temporal validity
~\cite{vu2023freshqa,meem2024pat,chen2021timeqa,chen2025chronoqa,kim2025tdbench}.
These studies motivate the problem; they are not evaluations of SCQA.

\subsection*{An evidence ladder separates established findings from open claims}

Table~\ref{tab:evidence-ladder} makes the claim boundary explicit. Within its
co-designed conformance suite, the study shows that seven applicability
invariants can be represented, executed and independently ablated under oracle
states. The audited model studies test, but do not support, a benefit of
explicit situation prompts; they locate the loss of applicability in missing
timestamps and dates instead.
