\subsection*{Situation prompting across four model families (E5, E6)}

The controlled results establish that the seven applicability invariants are
executable and causally separable (E1--E4); they do not show that
\emph{prompting} a language model for an explicit situation state improves it.
E5 tests this with three Gemini models, and a pre-registered replication (E6)
repeats it unchanged with three open-weight models from Meta, DeepSeek and
Qwen. Each model answers the same stratified sample (30 items per category,
$n=210$) with evidence, decoding and budget fixed, under a plain \emph{direct}
answer, a \emph{structured} JSON prompt or an explicit \emph{situation}-state
prompt (Methods). The primary quantity is the within-model paired
situation$-$structured contrast; every run passes the completion audit with no
failed calls, and E6 passes a multi-family audit.

\input{section/tables/e5_stats_table}

Explicit situation prompting does not improve on structured prompting
(Table~\ref{tab:e5-stats}, Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:e6-stats}). The
paired differences are $-2.9$, $-14.8$ and $-2.9$ percentage points in Gemini
and $-4.3$, $+19.5$ and $-0.5$ in E6, so the pre-registered criterion for an
improvement -- an interval above zero in at least two of the three E6 families
-- is not met. The one positive contrast (Llama, $+19.5$ [$+5.2$, $+36.2$]) is a
scoring artefact: Llama often wraps its structured JSON in prose or answers
with a boolean, which the pre-registered extractor misses. A post-hoc
sensitivity analysis that applies one more robust extractor to all six models
changes 47 Llama records and turns its contrast into $-1.9$ [$-7.1$, $+1.9$];
under it, situation prompting is never above structured prompting in any model
($-1.9$ to $-14.8$ points). Intervals are wide because the benchmark has only
seven template families (Methods). The situation prompt costs 2.7--6.2 times
the tokens of the direct prompt. On 12 SituatedQA temporal items the two
prompts differ by at most one item in every model, a smoke test only.

Removing metadata shows which variables matter (Extended Data
Tables~\ref{tab:e5-rag} and~\ref{tab:e6-rag}). Because each item has at most
three claims, the retrieval condition receives every claim, but as plain text
without timestamps, status, source, scope or observers. Temporal accuracy then
falls to $44.4\%$ in Gemini and to 23--50\% per E6 model, against 95.6--100\%
with metadata, as the pre-registered secondary hypothesis predicted in every
family, while relevant text alone suffices for most other categories. In v0.1,
where the decisive claim was always the newest, the same condition was the
strongest ($91.3\%$), an artefact that the balanced benchmark removes. A
tool-using agent, whose searches also omit timestamps, is weakest on temporal
items ($33.3\%$ in Gemini, $14.4\%$ in E6).

\subsection*{LLM sensors recover most situation cues (E7)}

Is the sensing gap specific to the rule-based sensor? In a second
pre-registered study (E7), four LLMs receive exactly that sensor's inputs --
query time, question and claim texts -- and fill the same state slots, which
drive the unchanged SCQA engine (Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:e7-sensor}).
On the same 210 items, end-to-end accuracy rises from $65.7\%$ for the rule
sensor to 79.0--87.1\%, and on non-temporal items from $71.1\%$ to
85.6--96.1\%; source-conflict items go from $0\%$ to $100\%$. Temporal items
stay at 33--40\% for every sensor, because the claim texts state no times.
Most of the rule sensor's gap is therefore specific to it; what remains is
information absent from the text, plus residual errors on observer knowledge
and missing premises. Claim-level slot scores track decisions poorly, as
vocabulary differences that leave the answer unchanged still count as errors.

Together, these studies separate two things that the oracle results cannot:
an explicit situation field adds nothing beyond structured prompting in any of
four model families, whereas stripping applicability metadata from otherwise
identical evidence is decisive for time-dependent items. Availability of the
right claims is not enough when their applicability cannot be read. These are
synthetic, single-benchmark results; independent natural-language evidence and
human agreement are still required (Methods).
