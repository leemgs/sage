\subsection*{A preliminary matched multi-model intervention (E5)}

The controlled results establish that the seven applicability invariants are
executable and causally separable (E1--E4); they do not show that
\emph{prompting} a language model for an explicit situation state improves it.
As a first, deliberately bounded test of that E5 claim, three Gemini models
answer a stratified, evidence-complete sample of SituationCatch-Bench
(30 items per category, $n=210$) with evidence, decoding and budget held fixed,
varying only the prompt: a plain \emph{direct} answer, a \emph{structured} JSON
prompt or an explicit \emph{situation}-state prompt (Methods). The primary
quantity is the within-model paired situation$-$structured contrast; the run
passes a completion audit with no failed calls.

\input{section/tables/e5_stats_table}

The result is null-to-negative (Table~\ref{tab:e5-stats}). Prompting for an
explicit situation state never improves on a matched structured prompt: the
paired situation$-$structured differences are $-2.9$, $-14.8$ and $-2.9$
percentage points. The $95\%$ cluster-bootstrap interval excludes zero only for
\texttt{gemini-2.5-flash-lite}, where the situation prompt is \emph{worse}, and
for \texttt{gemini-3.5-flash} its upper bound is zero. The intervals are wide
because the benchmark has only seven template families, so resampling
categories -- the honest unit of independence -- leaves little power for a
directional claim. Relative to the direct prompt, the situation prompt uses
2.7--3.0 times the provider-reported tokens and has higher median latency, with
no accuracy gain and no reduction in selective risk.

A small natural-language check points the same way. On a 12-item sample from the
public SituatedQA temporal split, the two date-anchored conditions differ by at
most one item (situation$-$structured differences of $0.0$, $0.0$ and $+8.3$
points across the three models). At $n=12$ this is a smoke test, not a result.

Retrieval shows which variables matter (Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:e5-rag}).
Because every item has at most three claims, the top-3 retrieval condition
receives all claims, in time order, but as plain text without timestamps,
status, source, scope or observer metadata. It reaches $79.4\%$ overall,
against $86.3\%$ structured and $79.5\%$ situation, and collapses on temporal
items ($44.4\%$ versus $95.6$--$97.8\%$ with metadata), where the answer
depends on when the query falls relative to each event. It is higher than the
full-evidence conditions on hidden-premise, counterfactual and scope items.
Removing the applicability metadata therefore harms the category whose answer
depends on it, while relevant text alone suffices elsewhere; given
category-level multiplicity these comparisons are exploratory. In v0.1, where
the decisive claim was always the newest, the same retrieval condition was the
strongest ($91.3\%$), an artefact that the balanced benchmark removes. A
tool-using agent that issues its own searches over the item's claims is the
weakest condition overall ($71.0\%$) and drops furthest on temporal items
($33.3\%$), because its search results likewise omit timestamps.

We read this as motivating the paper's distinction rather than as a plug-in
prompting win: a prompt-level ``situation'' field is not situation awareness.
Consistent with the controlled finding that accuracy collapses once states must
be \emph{sensed} from text ($100.0\%\rightarrow66.5\%$), the bottleneck is
situation sensing and validation, not a state slot in the prompt. This
single-provider, $n=210$ pilot neither confirms nor refutes the hypothesis at
scale; a confirmatory E5 requires multiple model families, a larger
natural-language set and human agreement (Methods).
