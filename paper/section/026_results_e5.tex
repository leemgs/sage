\subsection*{Situation prompting across seven model developers (E5, E6, E8a)}

The controlled results establish that the seven applicability invariants are
executable and causally separable (E1--E4); they do not show that
\emph{prompting} a language model for an explicit situation state improves it.
E5 tests this with three Gemini models, and a pre-registered replication (E6)
repeats it unchanged with three open-weight models from Meta, DeepSeek and
Qwen; a second pre-registered replication on free endpoints (E8a) adds models
from NVIDIA, rednote hi lab and Recursive. Each model answers the same stratified sample (30 items per category,
$n=210$) with evidence, decoding and budget fixed, under a plain \emph{direct}
answer, a \emph{structured} JSON prompt or an explicit \emph{situation}-state
prompt (Methods). The primary quantity is the within-model paired
situation$-$structured contrast; every run passes the completion audit with no
unresolved calls, and E6 and E8a each pass a multi-family audit.

\input{section/tables/e5_stats_table}

Explicit situation prompting does not improve on structured prompting
(Table~\ref{tab:e5-stats}, Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:e6-stats}). The
paired differences are $-2.9$, $-14.8$ and $-2.9$ percentage points in Gemini
and $-4.3$, $+19.5$ and $-0.5$ in E6, so the pre-registered criterion for an
improvement -- an interval above zero in at least two of the three E6 families
-- is not met. The one positive contrast (Llama, $+19.5$ [$+5.2$, $+36.2$]) is a
scoring artefact: the pre-registered extractor misses Llama's prose-wrapped and
boolean answers. A post-hoc sensitivity analysis with a more robust extractor
changes 47 Llama records and turns its contrast into $-1.9$ [$-7.1$, $+1.9$];
under it, situation prompting is never above structured prompting in these six models
($-1.9$ to $-14.8$ points). The situation prompt costs 2.7--6.2 times
the tokens of the direct prompt. E8a reaches the same verdict ($+0.5$, $-6.7$
and $-11.9$ points; Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:e8-stats}), also under the
sensitivity rescoring. Flint's negative contrast reflects responses cut off at
the output limit, which its mandatory reasoning shares; without them it is
$0.0$ [$-3.1$, $+2.3$] (post hoc; Methods). Nor does situation prompting detectably beat
chain-of-thought or self-reflection prompts (E10; Extended Data
Table~\ref{tab:e10-baselines}).

Removing metadata shows which variables matter (Extended Data
Tables~\ref{tab:e5-rag}, \ref{tab:e6-rag} and~\ref{tab:e8-rag}). Because each item has at most
three claims, the retrieval condition receives every claim, but as plain text
without timestamps, status, source, scope or observers. Temporal accuracy then
falls to $44.4\%$ in Gemini, to 23--50\% per E6 model and to 50.0--56.7\% per
E8a model, against 95.6--100\%
with metadata, as the pre-registered secondary hypothesis predicted in every
family, while relevant text alone suffices for most other categories. A
tool-using agent, whose searches also omit timestamps, is weakest on temporal
items ($33.3\%$ in Gemini, $14.4\%$ in E6).

\subsection*{LLM sensors recover most situation cues (E7)}

Is the sensing gap specific to the rule-based sensor? In a second
pre-registered study (E7), four LLMs receive exactly that sensor's inputs --
query time, question and claim texts -- and fill the same state slots, which
drive the unchanged SCQA engine (Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:e7-sensor}).
On the same 210 items, end-to-end accuracy rises from $65.7\%$ for the rule
sensor to 79.0--87.1\%, and on non-temporal items from $71.1\%$ to
85.6--96.1\%; source-conflict items go from $0\%$ to $100\%$. Temporal items
stay at 33--40\% for every sensor, because the claim texts state no times.
Most of the rule sensor's gap is therefore specific to it; what remains is
information absent from the text, plus residual errors on observer knowledge
and missing premises. Claim-level slot scores track decisions poorly, because
harmless vocabulary differences count as errors.

\subsection*{Natural-text temporal and geographic questions (E8b, E9)}

Two further pre-registered studies ask two E8a models independently written
SituatedQA~\cite{zhang2021situatedqa} questions without evidence;
the applicability variable is the query date (E8b, 300 temporal questions) or
location (E9, 297 geographic questions; Extended Data
Tables~\ref{tab:e8-nl} and~\ref{tab:e9-geo}). Dating an otherwise identical
question raises accuracy on time-dependent items by $+22.2$ [$+15.5$, $+28.8$]
and $+26.7$ [$+18.8$, $+34.0$] points, but not on control items dated at the
collection year ($-4.0$ in both, intervals including zero); naming the
location raises it by $+29.6$ [$+22.9$, $+36.2$] and $+40.7$ [$+33.2$,
$+48.3$] points. The situation prompt still never detectably beats
structured prompting ($-6.1$ to $+4.7$ points).

Together, these studies separate what the oracle results cannot: an explicit
situation field shows no detectable benefit over structured prompting in nine
models from seven developers, whereas the applicability variable itself --
timestamps on synthetic evidence, the date or place on natural questions -- is
decisive. Availability without readable applicability is not enough. Natural
text covers only the temporal and scope categories with two models; the other
five categories still require independent evidence
(Methods).
