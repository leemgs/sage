\section*{Methods}

\subsection*{Problem formulation}

An item consists of a query $q$, evidence claims $E$, a query time and, where
relevant, an observer or possible-world identifier. A claim may be supported in
isolation but inapplicable to the query. We define
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Apply}(e,q,S)\in\{\mathrm{valid},\mathrm{invalid},\mathrm{unresolved}\},
\end{equation}
where $S$ is the active situation state. The system first constructs or receives
$S$, then updates competing claims and selects an action from answering,
retrieval, clarification, abstention or task-specific action.

The end-to-end system is decomposed as
\begin{equation}
(q,E)\xrightarrow{f_{sense}}\hat S\xrightarrow{f_{update}}\hat S'
\xrightarrow{\pi}a\xrightarrow{g}y.
\end{equation}
This decomposition supports stage-specific evaluation. The gold-state condition
supplies $S$ directly and evaluates $f_{update}$, $\pi$ and the deterministic
answer renderer. A predicted-state condition applies a text-only deterministic
sensor before the same update and policy stages. It evaluates composition on
generated claim language, but not $f_{sense}$ on unrestricted natural language.

\subsection*{Relation to prior formalisms}

Situation engineering draws on several mature traditions but is not identical
to any one of them. Situation semantics treats meaning relative to partial
situations~\cite{barwise1983situations}. Situation and event calculi formalize
how actions change fluents over time~\cite{mccarthy1969problems,
kowalski1986event,reiter2001knowledge}. Temporal reasoning provides interval
relations and validity semantics~\cite{allen1983interval}. Belief revision and
truth-maintenance research asks how an agent should update inconsistent
commitments~\cite{alchourron1985logic,doyle1979truth}. POMDPs and dialogue-state
tracking maintain uncertainty over latent states for sequential decisions
~\cite{kaelbling1998pomdp,henderson2014second}. Temporal knowledge graphs and
bitemporal databases record changing facts and distinguish when a fact is valid
from when it is stored~\cite{snodgrass1992temporal}.

The proposed contribution is a systems-level applicability contract for
generative and agentic AI. It requires these representations to connect to
natural-language evidence, provenance, unresolved alternatives and a response
policy that can answer, retrieve, clarify, abstain or act. Thus the novelty is
not a new universal logic. It is the explicit decomposition of modern AI
reliability into (i) sensing a situation from heterogeneous evidence, (ii)
maintaining a provenance-linked state, (iii) validating applicability and
(iv) propagating unresolved state into behaviour. This framing allows existing
logical, probabilistic and neural methods to be compared through a common set of
interfaces and failure measurements.

A key distinction is that these traditions vary in whether they jointly expose
time, observer, possible world, uncertainty, provenance and an answer-or-action
gate; the proposed interface requires all of them to be reportable without
claiming that their underlying formalisms are new.

Dynamic epistemic logic and common-ground modelling formalize belief-changing
information and mutually recognized conversational commitments
~\cite{vanbenthem1996dynamic,stalnaker2002common}. Theory-of-mind models, world
models and active perception further address attributed beliefs, latent
environment state and information acquisition. These traditions reinforce rather
than eliminate the need for careful attribution: situation engineering does not
claim their theoretical results as new. Its proposed novelty is the systems
contract that exposes their state variables to provenance-aware
answer/retrieve/clarify/abstain gates in contemporary generative systems.

\subsection*{Situation schema}

The schema contains actors and entities; events and state transitions; valid
and observation time; scope; modality and source status; observer-specific
knowledge; possible-world identity; provenance; and unresolved answer-critical
variables. Update rules include temporal supersession, cancellation,
source-authority preference where specified, scope containment, observer
visibility and world separation. Unresolved variables are preserved rather than
converted into a guessed binary state.

\subsection*{SituationCatch-Bench generation}

The deterministic generator (seed 20260717) creates 600 instances for each of
seven categories using seeded lexical variation and shuffled claim order. Each
item includes the question, claims, structured situation fields, gold action,
gold answer and category. Version 0.2 balances every category exactly by item
index. Temporal queries fall after the last appointment or between the first
appointment and the resignation (300 each); hidden-premise questions omit the
user's region (300, gold \textsc{Clarify}) or state an eligible or ineligible
region (150 each); the rumour is newer than the official notice in half of the
source-conflict items; modality, scope, observer and counterfactual answers are
yes or no in 300 items each; and the undecided proposal (modality) and the
factual or hypothetical claim (counterfactual) is the newer claim in half of
the relevant items. Version 0.1 fixed one answer per category and always made
the decisive claim the most recent, so answer priors and recency solved most
categories; it is archived with all of its results. The categories and gold semantics are documented in the bundled
datasheet. Generation and evaluation are intentionally co-designed: the
benchmark is a conformance suite for explicit semantics, analogous to unit tests
for a software protocol, rather than an independent estimate of natural-language
performance.

\subsection*{Reference implementation}

SCQA is a deterministic Python reference implementation with no trained
parameters. It parses the supplied structured fields, performs category-specific
state updates and selects a permitted response. The three software-control
baselines each select one claim (the last one mentioned, the most recent one, or
the one sharing the most content words with the question after removing a fixed
English stop-word list) and read it at face value, ignoring query time, status,
source, scope, observers and world. These controls test whether the
specified state variables are necessary for the generated invariants; they are
not intended to represent competitive LLM, RAG or agent baselines.

\subsection*{Predicted- and corrupted-state evaluation}

The predicted-state sensor receives only claim text and infers event type,
actor, temporal expression, modality, source status and world or scope using
documented deterministic patterns, and reads a premise the user states about
themselves in the question (``I live in \ldots''); it cannot copy hidden gold
slots. The
downstream engine is unchanged. In a pre-specified corruption condition,
predicted source status is replaced by a generic reported source, modality is
forced to confirmed and scope is forced to global. All three conditions contain
the same 4,200 identifiers and are scored by exact action and answer. Item-level
and aggregate outputs are released in
\texttt{results/state\_conditions.csv} and
\texttt{results/state\_conditions\_summary.csv}.
The sensor is additionally scored claim by claim for actor, event, temporal
expression, validity interval, scope, modality, joint source/status,
observer-knowledge and world slots. Because claim order is preserved, this
comparison does not require learned alignment. The resulting files are
\texttt{results/predicted\_state\_slots.csv} and its aggregate summary.


\subsection*{Metrics and analysis}

The primary diagnostic measure is exact action-and-answer accuracy. For items
requiring clarification, correctness requires selecting clarification rather
than providing the latent answer. Category-level accuracy and matched component
ablations localize failure mechanisms. Because instances share template
families, item-level confidence intervals and $P$ values are not used as primary
scientific evidence in the revised manuscript.

Rule-derived confidence values are not treated as neural calibration.

\subsection*{Ablation design}

Seven ablations independently remove temporal validity, source status, modality,
scope, observer knowledge, world identity or missing-variable detection. Each
ablation is applied to all items, and its expected effect is specified before
execution. The purpose is to test causal alignment between representation and
failure category, not to estimate an average treatment effect in natural text.
An ablated component falls back to a situation-blind rule: removing time treats
every claim as current, removing source status or world identity selects the
most recent claim, removing modality or scope accepts a proposal or a local
claim as universal, removing observer knowledge assumes the person saw every
claim, and removing missing-variable detection assumes eligibility. In v0.1 the
source-status and world-identity ablations produced no errors because the
decisive claim was always the most recent; in v0.2 each of the seven ablations
produces exactly 300 errors, all in its own category. Baselines, category
results and ablations are regenerated by \texttt{code/run\_diagnostic.py}.

\subsection*{E5 multi-model pilot and confirmatory protocol}

The reported E5 pilot uses a stratified sample of 210 SituationCatch-Bench v0.2
items (30 per category; sampling seed 20260718) and three Gemini-family models:
\texttt{gemini-2.5-flash}, \texttt{gemini-2.5-flash-lite} and
\texttt{gemini-3.5-flash}, as in the v0.1 pilot. Because the native
API no longer serves the two 2.5 models to new projects, all three were reached
through OpenRouter (upstream: Google), with temperature zero, model reasoning
disabled and a common 1,024-token output limit; \texttt{gemini-3.5-flash} does
not allow disabling reasoning there and was run at the minimum reasoning effort.
Every recorded call used zero reasoning tokens, matching the thinking-off
setting of the v0.1 pilot. The v0.2 run kept the v0.1 conditions, primary
contrast, scoring rule and statistical analysis unchanged; only the benchmark
and the access route differ. Because hosted models can change behind a fixed name,
exact reproduction rests on the archived responses. Each model receives identical evidence under direct,
structured and explicit-situation prompts. A separate retrieval condition
selects the top three claims by token overlap with the question and supplies
their plain text, in time order but without timestamps or other metadata, to
the direct-answer prompt; because no item has more than three claims, this
condition differs from the direct prompt only in omitting that metadata. A tool-using agent condition
follows a ReAct-style loop: the model may issue \texttt{search} queries over the
item's claims, receives the matching claims, and then answers. All requests, responses,
tokens, latencies and failures are archived. The
completion gate joins records to the frozen sample, rejects missing or
unexpected identifiers and requires 30 successful responses in every category
for every requested model and condition. The reported run contains no failed
calls and passes this gate. Scoring credits \textsc{Clarify}/\textsc{Abstain}
actions uniformly in plain text and JSON.

Accuracy contrasts use category-clustered bootstrap intervals (3,000 resamples,
seed 20260726), treating the seven template categories as clusters. These
intervals quantify sensitivity across the available template families, not
population uncertainty over unrestricted questions. With only seven clusters the
percentile intervals are approximate and can lie on a boundary (for example an
upper bound of exactly zero), so they are reported descriptively rather than as
significance tests. Cost summaries report
provider token counts and measured request latency by condition. An archived
12-item SituatedQA smoke test is superseded by E8b and not analysed. The frozen
manifest, model snapshots and cost accounting are in
\texttt{code/experiment\_manifest.e5.json}, and the audited run is reproduced
with \texttt{python code/run\_e5.py}.

Protocols for a larger confirmatory study, external generalization, deployment
evaluation and reporting, which have not been executed, are specified in
Supplementary Notes 14 and 15.

\subsection*{Pre-registered multi-family replication (E6) and LLM sensors (E7)}

Both studies were pre-registered in the repository
(\texttt{paper/PREREGISTRATION\_E6\_E7.md}) before any study call; commits
timestamp the design and one amendment. E6 repeats E5 with identical
code, prompts, sample, decoding and conditions in three further developer
families, reached through OpenRouter with reasoning disabled:
\texttt{llama-4-maverick} (Meta), \texttt{deepseek-v4.1-flash} (DeepSeek) and
\texttt{qwen3.8-flash} (Qwen), all with open weights. The amendment, made
before any outcome was inspected, replaced the originally planned Anthropic
and OpenAI models after the API credit ran out; proprietary families other
than Gemini are therefore not covered. The pre-specified decision rule is that
explicit situation prompting is said to improve on structured prompting only if
the paired 95\% cluster-bootstrap interval lies above zero in at least two of
the three E6 families. A multi-family audit counts families by model developer
and requires at least three.

E7 asks whether the sensing gap of the rule-based sensor is specific to it. An
LLM receives exactly the rule sensor's inputs for each of the 210 sample items
-- the query time, the question and the claim texts, with no metadata -- and
returns the engine's state slots (event, actor, status, source, scope,
observers, stated times and the user's region) from fixed vocabularies. The
predicted state is passed unchanged to the SCQA engine, and slots are scored
with the rule sensor's scorer, made case-insensitive and with actors matched by
whole word for both sensors alike. An invalid output is retried once and then
scored as a sensing failure. Claim texts contain no timestamps, so the time
slot is unrecoverable by design for every sensor.

Two analyses were added after the data were seen and are reported as such.
First, the pre-registered extractor parses a JSON answer only when the whole
response is JSON and does not map a boolean answer to yes or no; Llama often
wrote prose before its JSON or answered \texttt{"answer": false}. A
sensitivity analysis (\texttt{code/sensitivity\_rescore.py}) applies one more
robust extractor -- the last JSON object in the response, booleans mapped to
yes/no -- to every record of all six E5/E6 models alike, and later to the three
E8a models, without altering the
archived files; the pre-registered results remain primary. Second, E7's
interpretation rule did not fit the data cleanly (the shortfall lay mainly in
the unrecoverable time slot, yet claim-level cue-slot accuracy was 69--76\%),
so we report all pre-registered measures and add the split of end-to-end
accuracy into temporal and other items. The audit was corrected before analysis to count developers, not gateways.

\subsection*{Pre-registered free-tier replication (E8a) and natural-text study (E8b)}

This study had no funding, so E8 uses only zero-price OpenRouter endpoints;
every successful call reports a cost of zero. The design
(\texttt{paper/PREREGISTRATION\_E8.md}) was committed before any study call,
with one amendment made before any outcome was computed. E8a repeats E6
unchanged (direct, structured, situation and no-metadata retrieval; no agent)
with \texttt{nemotron-3-ultra-550b-a55b} (NVIDIA),
\texttt{dots-3-note-preview} (rednote hi lab) and \texttt{flint-v1.0}
(Recursive), under E6's decision rule. The amendment replaced a planned model
that was withdrawn mid-run; its partial records are archived and excluded.
Flint's endpoint refuses to disable reasoning, so it ran with minimal reasoning
effort, and its reasoning tokens share the 1,024-token output budget. Daily
free quotas were handled by stopping and resuming; transient errors were retried
until each item and condition had one success, and no item was imputed,
substituted or dropped.

E8b samples 300 questions from the SituatedQA temporal test
split~\cite{zhang2021situatedqa} (75 per date type, fixed seed; upstream commit
and file hash in the pre-registration) and asks two E8a models each question
undated with the direct prompt and dated with the direct, structured and
situation prompts. The date types are the dataset's collection year (which we
use as the control), the start of an answer's validity, and a sampled year or
date. A response is correct if it contains any gold answer as a whole word, which misses correct paraphrases in every condition alike.
Contrasts use a bootstrap clustered on the upstream question (255 clusters,
3,000 resamples). The upstream repository states no licence, so question texts
are not redistributed: archived records keep item identifiers and responses,
and a script rebuilds the sample from a clone of the source.

One analysis was added after the data were seen and is labelled post hoc: a
response that reaches the output limit is cut off, and when it is not valid
JSON the pre-registered extractor falls back to the raw text, so a gold name
that appears in the restated evidence can be credited. We therefore report how
many responses reach the limit and the contrast on items where neither
response does (\texttt{code/e8\_truncation.py}); only Flint is affected
(no more than one E8b response per model reaches the limit).

E10 (\texttt{paper/PREREGISTRATION\_E10.md}, committed before any call) adds the
chain-of-thought and self-reflection prompts, unchanged since E5, for the three
E8a models on the 210 items, and compares them with the archived E8a situation
records under E6's decision rule; the new conditions were collected 3--8 days
after the E8a records. Few-shot prompting (prompts would be written after seeing
outputs) and self-consistency (free quota) were not run. Flint again reaches the output limit often
(94 of 210 self-reflection and 38 of 210 chain-of-thought responses).

E9 (\texttt{paper/PREREGISTRATION\_E9.md}, committed before any call) repeats the
E8b design on the SituatedQA geographic test split~\cite{zhang2021situatedqa}: 300
items drawn uniformly (fixed seed) from 125 source questions, each asked unlocated
with the direct prompt and located with the direct, structured and situation
prompts, with the two E8b models. There is no control stratum, because every
unlocated question is ambiguous across locations; a split into US-located and other
items is reported descriptively. One amendment, made before any outcome was
computed, excludes the three items of one source question (firearm permits) that
the dots endpoint rejected on every attempt, so both models are compared on the
same 297 items; Nemotron on all 300 items gives the same result. Many gold answers
are long or numeric, so absolute accuracy is low in every condition.

\subsection*{Human annotation}

The annotation program gives each annotator a separately shuffled, blinded
packet of a 70-item stratified subset and computes Fleiss' $\kappa$ and
unanimous agreement per slot only from complete, frozen files
(\texttt{paper/annotation\_packets/}).

Three volunteer annotators, recruited by the author and not involved in the
study, each completed one packet independently (no compensation; the author
reports that institutional ethics review was not required; no personal data
were collected). Packets showed the query time and the evidence as readable
lines, offered only codebook values, and were accompanied by seven worked
examples that are not among the 70 items. Before scoring, two blank answers
whose action was ABSTAIN were filled with ABSTAIN as the codebook prescribes,
and one answer cell that was empty in a returned file, although the annotator
reported having filled it, was obtained afterwards from that annotator through
the author, without showing the other labels. Fleiss'
$\kappa$ is 0.92 for possible world, 0.72 for answer, 0.49 for source status,
0.43 for observer state, 0.36 for scope, 0.25 for action, 0.20 for modality
and $-0.07$ for temporal relevance (Supplementary Note 10). One annotator
marked almost every item as time-dependent, apparently reading any time-stamped
evidence that way, whereas the codebook intends whether the answer would change
with the query time; this item is ambiguous as worded. Descriptively, the majority label
matches the reference label implied by the generated state on 97--100\% of
items for every slot except temporal relevance (63\%). Because the worked
examples state which slot usually decides each category, agreement with the
reference partly reflects the instructions. Labels, agreement and the
unadjudicated disagreement list are in \texttt{paper/results/human\_iaa/}.
A synthetic persona run, machine-marked as not human evidence, only tests the
software.

\subsection*{Ethics and use of generative AI}

The controlled benchmark contains no personal records. The only human
participants were the three adult volunteer annotators, who labelled synthetic
benchmark items, agreed to take part, were not compensated and provided no
personal data; the author reports that institutional ethics review was not
required for this activity. Their labels are released without names.
Generative AI assisted drafting and software development. The author inspected
the code, generated outputs, calculations and references and remains responsible
for the manuscript. No empirical values were fabricated to represent unexecuted
model calls or human studies.
